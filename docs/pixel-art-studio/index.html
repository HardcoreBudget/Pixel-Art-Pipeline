Pixel Art Studio for Unity · trailer breakdown

Pixel Art Studio Trailer

Two trailers. The 2.0 trailer comes first: 88 seconds, six new levels on a World 2 map. The 1.x trailer below it is 104 seconds at 480 × 270. Nine characters, six stages and a UI kit were built as .pas documents by agents working through the plugin's own verbs, then scripted beat by beat, recorded in Unity and checked pixel for pixel. The map is a five-zone hub that unlocks level by level, with a preview of each level, and the fifth level, a haunted archive, shows scene lights in three render pipelines.

The script

Thirteen scene files, compiled together into one trailer.json: 599 beats, 67 text rows, 71 audio slots and 280 rows on the cue sheet. A beat is one line of JSON: a camera cut or wipe, a tag to play, layers to hide or show, a palette to swap to, a move, a frozen clock, or a line of dialogue. Click a scene on the bar above to jump the trailer to it.

Final version

480 × 272, 539 unique tiles, a 184-entry Indexed palette with one ramp per zone. Five zones tile the whole canvas, with no open meadow between them, and every border has a perimeter that belongs to one side. The path and the five stone discs stay put. 1-1 is a castle courtyard around the lake, which becomes its moat, with a drawbridge where the path crosses. 1-2 is a rooftop city with a water tower, a blinking neon sign and a retaining wall to the south. 1-3 is the arena at night, with braziers. 1-4 is a sketchbook with a torn paper edge all round. 1-5 is the haunted Archive behind an iron fence and a fog line, with a crypt court, candles, graves and dead trees.

The hub unlocks as you go

The map starts locked. Each zone has a lock document: a flat three-tone silhouette of its landmarks, with fog banks drifting over it (a fog tag of 12 frames at 100 ms). After each level the trailer returns to the map for a second and a half. The next path draws itself (a draw tag of 13 short frames, with a bright tip), the zone floods with colour from the end of the path outward (a flood tag of 10 frames at 40 ms, with a bright front), and its named preview pops open. Zones already cleared show their stars. Every overlay is cut from the real renders of the stage, so a pixel of an overlay equals the stage pixel it stands for.

A preview for every zone

When a zone unlocks, a framed window opens over the map with that level's own art at 1:1. Each preview is a .pas document of 112 × 99 pixels with an open tag (the window grows from a line) and a loop tag, and its characters are frames rendered by the plugin. Shown at x4.

Behind the scenes: the real window

A 4:07 tour of the Studio window as you get it in Unity: the window itself, Bonk built step by step, every drawing tool at real speed, palettes, animation, tiles, scene lights, an agent driving the open document, and how the trailer above was assembled. Where the plugin fell short

While they built the trailer, the agents wrote down every place where the plugin's verbs were not enough and they had to fall back to a script of their own: 136 notes in all. 86 of them asked for a new verb and 22 for a clearer explanation in the plugin's SKILL.md. The first 40 became a round of new plugin features, among them the verbs that peel a design, clean a frame, grow hidden overlap and check a pose, and the plugin now has 85 verbs (67 commands and 18 queries).
